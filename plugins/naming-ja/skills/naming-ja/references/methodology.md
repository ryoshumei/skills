# Styles, techniques and the six lenses

Contents: naming styles (8), generation techniques, the six lenses (六感), sound notes for Japanese, health-product traps.

## Naming styles

Each style says what it does well, what goes wrong, and how to generate names in it. The well-known examples are only for calibration.

### 1. カタカナ造語: a Japanese word, cut or respelled in katakana

Katakana marks an everyday word as a name and turns it into a coinage. アサヒ and キリン write a Japanese word in katakana, and マツキヨ cuts マツモトキヨシ down.

- **Good:** short and distinct, and it still carries meaning.
- **Risk:** the obvious pun is often taken already, and it can read as a foreign word.
- **Generate:** cut a phrase to 3–4 morae, respell a word users already say, or swap one mora.

### 2. 和語: native words, usually in hiragana

These are soft, warm and old: しるべ (a guidepost), よすが (something to hold on to), あかし (proof), ことづて (a message passed on), なごみ (calm).

- **Good:** warmth, and it reads as a companion.
- **Risk:** a plain noun is hard to find in search and hard to register, and some sound dated.
- **Generate:** 古語 and 雅語, nouns made from verbs (控え, 添え, 書き留め), and compounds (より + どころ).

### 3. 漢字・漢語: one or two kanji, or a kanji coinage

These are strong on a seal and an icon. Examples are 綾鷹 and 爽健美茶.

- **Good:** dense meaning that fits a 印.
- **Risk:** readings split (もり or まもり), and they can feel official or medical. Chinese readers also see a Chinese meaning.
- **Generate:** pair a promise kanji (証, 記, 添, 標) with an object (言, 薬, 日). Count strokes, because a small seal blurs dense kanji.

### 4. 口ぐせ・話し言葉: the user's own phrase

Turn what people say into the name, as お〜いお茶 does. For a product that fixes a pain, the name can be the "before" and the product the "after".

- **Good:** instantly relatable, and it tells a story in one word.
- **Risk:** it can sound like hesitation or doubt, and it usually needs katakana to read as a name.
- **Generate:** list what people say before, during and after a visit (なんだっけ, えっと, たしか, 聞きそびれた), then shape each phrase into a name.

### 5. 掛詞・ダジャレ: two meanings at once

ドコモ is both どこも ("everywhere") and DOCOMO ("do communications over the mobile network").

- **Good:** memorable, and it rewards a second look.
- **Risk:** a groan, or one of the two meanings doesn't land.
- **Generate:** find a word in the product's world that has a homophone, such as 書き留め and 書留.

### 6. 擬音語・擬態語: sound and feeling words

- **Good:** friendly and easy to say.
- **Risk:** it can feel childish for a health product, and it's hard to register.
- **Generate:** words for calm, clarity and holding on (すっと, ほっと, ぴたり, そっと), or doubling (メモメモ).

### 7. 外来語・造語・ローマ字: loanwords, roots from other languages, blends, Latin letters

Examples: メルカリ (Latin mercari, "to trade"), ヤクルト (Esperanto jahurto, "yogurt"), カロリーメイト (calorie + mate), ぐるなび (グルメ + ナビ). Some brands are written in Latin letters, such as freee and SmartHR.

- **Good:** modern, with room to grow, and easier to register.
- **Risk:** opaque to older users. Check the meaning in English and in the source language.
- **Generate:** find Latin, Greek or Esperanto roots for source, trace, companion and memory, and blend them with Japanese.
- **Latin letters:** give every Latin-letter name its katakana reading. The counter test applies to that reading, because people will say it, not spell it.

### 8. 名前・キャラクター: a persona's name

A name you would call a person or a small companion.

- **Good:** warmth, and it's natural in conversation (「〇〇に聞いてみる」).
- **Risk:** it reads as a chatbot mascot, it may collide with real people's names, and it can undercut seriousness.
- **Generate:** short given names, nicknames, and 〜ちゃん or 〜さん forms. Check how each one sounds next to 先生.

Show each candidate in its best script. Add a second script when it changes the feel: あさひ, アサヒ and 朝日 all read differently.

## Generation techniques

- **Scene map:** start from the moments: the waiting room, just after the exam, the pharmacy counter, home that night. List the objects, actions and feelings in each scene, and name from those.
- **Word mining:** use synonym dictionaries, 古語辞典, regional words (check what they mean in other regions) and the trade's own words (薬局, 手帳, 控え, 付箋, しおり).
- **Word shaping:** cut a word (マツキヨ), make a noun from a verb (なごみ from なごむ), join two words (いろはす is いろは + LOHAS), change the ending (〜り, 〜ね, 〜か, 〜と), double a word (メモメモ), or respell it in katakana or romaji.
- **In-context test:** put the name in real sentences: 「〇〇に聞いてみる」「〇〇で先生に見せる」「〇〇しといた」. Drop names that sound wrong in all three.

## The six lenses (六感)

Score each lens from 1 to 5. The totals only explain trade-offs.

| Lens | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **見た目** (look) | long or cluttered, or mixed scripts | clean, 5–6 characters | 2–4 characters, clear on an icon and in a small seal (for example 22 px) |
| **音** (sound) | hard to say, or easily misheard | easy to say | a pleasant rhythm that passes the counter test |
| **意味** (meaning) | a wrong or risky sense in Japanese, Chinese or English | neutral | carries the promise without overclaiming |
| **覚えやすさ** (memory) | forgettable, blends in | memorable with effort | recalled the morning after one hearing |
| **使い勝手** (use) | hard to type, or a common word that search buries | works | converts in one IME step, can be found in App Store search, and works as a verb and an @handle |
| **広がり** (growth) | locked to one feature | neutral | still fits family members, other clinics and new features |

- **Counter test:** say the name at a pharmacy counter or on the phone. Can the listener write it down on the first try?
- **Morning-after test:** after hearing it once, can someone recall it the next day?

## Sound notes for Japanese

- Names of 2–4 morae are the easiest to say and remember. ー, っ and ん each count as one mora.
- な・ま・や・わ行 sound soft, and か・さ・た行 sound crisp. Voiced sounds (が・ざ・だ・ば) sound heavier, and ぱ行 sounds playful.
- In a health product, avoid names that put し (死) or く (苦) in a prominent spot, and names that echo illness words (病, 痛, 苦).
- Say the name aloud with its pitch pattern. A name that shares its sounds and pitch with a common word gets misheard as that word.

## Health-product traps

A patient reads the name at a worried moment, and doctors and pharmacists see it too. Screen every finalist against these traps:

- **Promises:** diagnosis, cure, miracles or certainty (確定, 診断, 治る, 癒えた, 奇跡, 確か as a claim). Tie any certainty to the source instead: 「確かめられる」, not 「確かです」.
- **Dependence:** names meaning "something to lean on" (よりどころ, たより) can read badly to people on psychiatric medication.
- **Surveillance:** evidence or recording words (証拠, 録音, 監視) can make doctors say less.
- **Death and disaster:** echoes of death, accidents or disasters have no place in a health app.
- **Stigma:** 手帳 can call up the disability card (障害者手帳).
- **Over-trust:** a human name or a doctor-like persona invites people to trust the app like a clinician.
- **Hiding:** a name about not showing things needs the safety story beside it, such as a warning when a hidden medicine matters.
- **Borrowed words:** words that the product's own sources or competitors already use as names or verbs. For example, a medication app may quote くすりのしおり, a public series of drug leaflets.
